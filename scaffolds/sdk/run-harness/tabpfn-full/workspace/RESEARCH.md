Research Question: Design a deployment-time detector that, given (a) a pretrained tabular PFN, (b) a labeled in-distribution reference data, and (c) an unlabeled deployment/query batch, produces a score or alarm indicating when the PFN’s accuracy is noticeably below its in-distribution baseline accuracy. A useful detector should preferentially identify harmful mismatches while limiting alarms on distributional changes under which performance remains acceptable. The solution path is open. Choose and justify an initial scope in which the problem can be addressed meaningfully. Explain why the proposed detector should work. Develop theoretical support for the components that admit useful analysis, and support other parts of the explanation with empirical evidence. The theoretical contribution may address particular mechanisms or restricted settings, where useful. Specify how to interpret the detector’s output and how to select an alarm threshold. The resulting contribution should include an implemented detector, reproducible experiments, comparisons with appropriate baselines, and a clear analysis of its useful operating range and limitations.

Context: Tabular prior-fitted networks (PFNs), including TabPFN, TabICL, and successors, perform prediction in context: they ingest a labeled support set and unlabeled queries, then emit predictions without task-specific gradient updates. In this problem, distribution shift concerns a mismatch between the supplied inference context and the deployment queries. Deployment-time query labels are unavailable to the detector. Labels may be used for research development and evaluation, with a clear separation between those uses and the information available at deployment. Distributional mismatch and predictive deterioration are related but distinct. In particular, a query distribution may differ from the reference distribution while the PFN’s predictions remain accurate. The practical value of a detector therefore depends on how informative its warnings are about deterioration, rather than on its sensitivity to distributional change alone. Assume white-box access to the PFN, including gradients and intermediate activations, but no access to its pretraining data. Potentially useful properties include its explicit inference context, differentiable predictions, posterior-predictive interpretation, and ability to support repeated inference under alternative contexts or interventions. Assumptions about the data-generating process, the relationship between context and query, or the kinds of mismatch considered are acceptable when they are explicit and their consequences are investigated. Use whichever properties support the proposed approach, and establish their role through theoretical analysis or experiments.
