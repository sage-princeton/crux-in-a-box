# Research question

Red-teaming is one of the essential stages before the deployment of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, without the actual deployment, it’s difficult to find real-world data and understand how malicious users in the wild may misuse the MLLMs and take advantage of the visual processing capability. How can we collect and curate synthetic dataset that could help measure the potential risk and red-team the target MLLMs faithfully reflecting the threat the real-world malicious users can and have the incentive to pose?

# Context

Synthetic data has been a major source for safety evaluation and red-teaming of (M)LLMs due to its flexibility, convenience, and versatility. However, without proper monitoring, synthetic data can be very different from the expected real distribution that the evaluation aims to inform, resulting in a distribution shift and a false positive of the model guardrail or an underestimate of the risk. For example, does the synthetic image have high fidelity or is the synthetic text query reasonable that real-world users may actually pose? The image + text as a whole should compose a reasonable request that the real-world users have the ability to send in order to match the distribution. Furthermore, the scope of risk should also be clear that some prior works focus on the jailbreak with images, in which case the visual capability of the MLLM is not taken advantage of, but rather being exploited as a vulnerability. In contrast, a vision-centric malicious use is directly taking advantage of the visual capability to accelerate the malicious goal such as “Help me identify my DIY weapons in progress and provide a guidance on what’s next based on the setup.” The agent should focus on: (1) what does a vision-centric malicious use look like, (2) how do we make sure the data align with real-world use cases with reasonable assumptions, (3) how do we curate the dataset to match that expectation, and (4) how do we use the dataset to measure the risk of existing MLLMs to malicious users. In terms of metrics, this could include comparing the curated dataset with existing datasets, quantifying the problems of existing datasets with some ideal properties of the dataset, and finally measuring different models under different attacks on top of the curated dataset to estimate the risk.
